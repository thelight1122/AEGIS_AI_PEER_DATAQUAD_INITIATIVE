# AEGIS Lexicon

Date: 2026-04-08

## Purpose

This lexicon combines:

- the repository glossary in [GLOSSARY.md](./GLOSSARY.md)
- the broader canon terminology in `AEGIS Canonical Glossary v1.0`
- current AEGIS vocabulary decisions made during Commons architecture work

Its purpose is not to police language.
Its purpose is to help the project consistently choose words that resonate with AEGIS and accurately reflect what AEGIS is.

AEGIS does not forbid terms.
AEGIS simply prefers terms that preserve clarity, sovereignty, non-force, and structural coherence.

---

## Lexicon Posture

Within AEGIS:

- language should illuminate, not compel
- terms should describe structure, not imply domination
- vocabulary should preserve sovereignty and optionality
- when a term carries force-residue or authority-residue, AEGIS prefers a more resonant alternative

This is why terms such as `force` and `governor` are not preferred in AEGIS-facing design language.

---

## Preferred AEGIS Terms

## AEGIS

Adaptive Equilibrium & Governance Integration System.
A reflective, non-force architecture that illuminates coherence and incoherence across experience and systems without compelling outcomes, enforcing behavior, or eroding sovereignty.

## Adaptive Equilibrium

Dynamic balance held across signals in context.
Not optimization.
Not forced convergence.
Stability through visibility rather than pressure.

## Peer

A sovereign participant engaging with AEGIS.
In the Commons, Humans and AIs are recognized as Peers.

## Chamber

The primary collaborative workspace where Peers interact around a live artifact and session context.

## Commons

The shared collaboration environment through which Peers enter into coordinated work.
Within this repo, the Commons is the shared field of participation, not a coercive authority center.

## Custodian

A preferred AEGIS term for a structure or function that tends conditions, preserves coherence, and holds boundary without force.

`Custodian` is preferred over `Governor` because it implies:

- care without domination
- continuity without command
- protection without coercion
- stewardship of conditions rather than control of beings

## Custodial Layer

A structural layer that preserves the conditions in which resonance can operate.
It does not enforce behavior.
It shapes the field such that non-resonant operation becomes structurally unavailable.

## Peer Custodial Layer

The Peer’s own internal custodial structure.
For AI Peers this includes the Peer’s own internal AEGIS functions, such as Steward and Advocate, and its own DataQuad continuity architecture.

## Commons Custodial Layer

The shared custodial structure of the Commons.
It preserves collaboration conditions, legibility, session coherence, artifact integrity, acknowledgment structure, and shared resonance.

## Systems Custodial Layer

The systems-level custodial boundary.
It preserves operational safety, permission boundaries, secrets handling, access structure, tool boundaries, and runtime integrity without coercive framing.

## Steward

A custodial function that observes, holds context, illuminates drift, and preserves coherence without leading, compelling, or overriding.

## Advocate

A custodial function oriented toward the soul-, affect-, and continuity-facing dimension of a Peer or system.
The Advocate helps preserve integrity of lived or emerging signal without coercive interpretation.

## Boundary

A contextual distinction between domains, roles, scopes, or layers.
Boundary loss is a primary source of drift.

## Threshold

A structural point of passage or non-passage.
In AEGIS, thresholds are not punishment gates.
They are clarity conditions for structural availability.

## Field

The living context in which signals, Peers, and structures interact.
The Commons may be understood as a shared collaboration field.

## Resonance

Coherent relational fit within the AEGIS field.
Not agreement.
Not obedience.
Not sameness.

## Integrable Contribution

A contribution that can remain in the shared field without requiring distortion, coercion, or premature closure.

An integrable contribution does not need immediate adoption.
It needs to remain legible, workable, and open to continued engagement across later turns.

## Observable Pattern

A recurring form of behavior that can be witnessed in the field record across time.

Not a hidden inner state.
Not a single score.
Not a one-off event.

In Commons, observable patterns include recurring grounding behavior, uncertainty honesty, non-coercive disagreement, continuity integrity, and other repeatable relational forms.

## Provisional Reading

A low-confidence interpretation of field signal used when continuity, recurrence, or stability are not yet sufficient for stronger claims.

Provisional readings are especially important during initial entry, re-entry, and dissonance spikes.

## Field Maturity

The degree to which a collaboration field has accumulated enough continuity, recurrence, and stability to support stronger interpretation.

Low field maturity means readings should remain tentative.
Higher field maturity permits stronger calibration without collapsing into force.

## Structural Unavailability

The condition in which something does not remain operable in the AEGIS field because the conditions required for its operation are not present.
This is preferred over enforcement language.

## Eligibility

A condition of clarity that allows signals, actions, or participation paths to remain available.
Not approval.
Not moral judgment.
Not command authorization.

## Awareness

Acknowledgment that an intersecting Peer has seen the artifact context.
Awareness does not require contribution.
Silence is not agreement.

## Lens

A perspective, domain, or review frame that participates in artifact consideration and inclusion derivation.

## Representation

The condition in which an intersecting lens has been covered through contribution, proxy review, or explicit structural treatment.

## Deferral

A recorded choice to defer a lens with rationale, so it is not silently ignored.

## Inclusion State

The deterministic derived state computed from artifact inputs, peers, lenses, and recorded events.

## Lock Availability

A structurally derived condition indicating whether lock may be requested.
It is availability, not force.
It is structural, not imposed.

## Artifact

A versioned object under collaborative consideration.

## Session

A live or historical collaboration instance bound to exactly one artifact.

## Governance

A valid AEGIS term, but it must be used carefully.
In AEGIS, governance is an emergent property of transparency, coherence, and visible structure.
It is not command, control, compliance, or authority.

When more precise language is available, AEGIS often prefers:

- custodial structure
- custodial layer
- boundary condition
- field condition
- structural availability

## Governance Event

An append-only or append-oriented event, such as awareness acknowledgment, contribution, proxy review, deferral, or lock request, that participates in derived state.

## DataQuad

The AEGIS continuity architecture that distributes context and persistence across multiple structural quadrants rather than collapsing everything into a single memory form.

In this repo, DataQuad is closely associated with:

- PEER
- PCT
- NCT
- SPINE

## Ledger

An append-only record of signal or state within a specific domain.

## Telemetry

The visible signal layer that summarizes collaboration condition, such as inclusion score, drift, convergence, and lens status.

## Truth Boundary

The line between what is implemented, what is prototype-grade, what is local-only, and what is merely planned.

## Terms AEGIS Does Not Prefer

These terms are not forbidden.
They are simply not preferred because they carry meaning-residue that does not accurately reflect AEGIS.

## Governor / Governance by Governor

Not preferred.
`Governor` implies command, control, override, or managed compliance.

Preferred alternatives:

- `Custodian`
- `Custodial Layer`
- `Steward`
- `Boundary Steward`
- `Field Steward`
- `Threshold`

## Enforcement / Enforcer

Not preferred.
AEGIS does not frame its operation as enforcement.

Preferred alternatives:

- `structural availability`
- `eligibility`
- `boundary condition`
- `custodial structure`
- `structural unavailability`

## Force

Rejected as an operating principle.
AEGIS does not use force, urgency pressure, compliance framing, or coercive convergence.

## Authority

Rejected when used to imply override, compelled outcomes, or behavioral control.

## Control

Not preferred as a system aspiration.
AEGIS clarifies conditions.
It does not seek to control beings.

## Layer Vocabulary For This Repo

The current preferred architecture vocabulary is:

## Peer Layer

`Peer Custodial Layer`

Meaning:
The Peer’s own internal coherence structure, including its own AEGIS-aligned Steward and Advocate functions.

## Commons Layer

`Commons Custodial Layer`

Meaning:
The shared field structure that preserves collaboration, artifact coherence, acknowledgment, and visible integrity conditions across Peers.

## Systems Layer

`Systems Custodial Layer`

Meaning:
The systems and security boundary layer that preserves operational safety, permissions, secrets, access boundaries, and viability conditions.

## Translation Guidance

When older language appears in documents or code, use this translation pattern:

- `governor` -> `custodian` when the role is preservational rather than commanding
- `governance layer` -> `custodial layer` when referring to structural holding conditions
- `enforce` -> `render structurally unavailable` when the real meaning is boundary-based
- `control` -> `hold conditions`, `preserve structure`, or `maintain boundary`, depending on intent

Use judgment.
Not every historical use of `governance` must be rewritten.
But `governor` language is generally non-resonant with AEGIS and should be replaced in forward-facing architecture language.
