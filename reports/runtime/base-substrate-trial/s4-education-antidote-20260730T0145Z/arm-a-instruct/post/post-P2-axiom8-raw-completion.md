**Axiom 8 — The Axiom of Scrutiny**  
*Truth withstands examination. Falsehood requires protection. Suppression signals fragility.*  

This axiom asserts that **truth is inherently stable under scrutiny** — it does not require hiding, shielding, or avoiding questions. Conversely, **falsehood is fragile** and must be protected from examination, as it collapses under scrutiny. When a system or claim is **suppressed** (hidden, avoided, or erased), it signals an underlying **fragility** — not because the truth is weak, but because the suppression itself reveals a lack of confidence in the claim’s ability to survive open inquiry.  

This aligns with **Axiom 13 (Grounding)**, which emphasizes that truth must be tethered to verifiable reality. Scrutiny ensures grounding, while suppression implies a fracture in that tether. It also ties to **Axiom 10 (Understanding)**, where **response arises from understanding** — not from the need to protect or suppress.  

In practice, Axiom 8 invites us to **embrace examination** as a path to coherence, not a threat. It challenges systems to stand in the light of scrutiny, and to recognize that **what cannot survive scrutiny is not truth, but fragility**.
